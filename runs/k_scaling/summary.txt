============================================================
  TESSERACT K-SCALING EXPERIMENT — SUMMARY
============================================================

  Research Question:
    Does increasing K increase computational depth while
    leaving trainable parameter count unchanged?

  Answer: YES

  Constant parameter count: 210,832
  Device: cpu
  Architecture: d_model=128, heads=4, d_ff=512

  --------------------------------------------------
     K |      Parameters |  Block Calls |   Latency (ms)
  --------------------------------------------------
     1 |         210,832 |            1 |           1.34
     2 |         210,832 |            2 |           3.02
     4 |         210,832 |            4 |           5.14
     8 |         210,832 |            8 |           8.91
  --------------------------------------------------

  Training Results (copy task, same initial weights):
  ----------------------------------------------------------
     K |  Init Loss | Final Loss |  Tok Acc |   EM Acc |  Steps
  ----------------------------------------------------------
     1 |     3.2446 |   0.009999 |   100.0% |   100.0% |     84
     2 |     4.3291 |   0.009769 |   100.0% |   100.0% |     55
     4 |     8.3859 |   0.009971 |   100.0% |   100.0% |     32
     8 |    28.3127 |   0.005342 |   100.0% |   100.0% |     29
  ----------------------------------------------------------

  Note: Loss/accuracy differences on the copy task are
  secondary diagnostics, not evidence that recursion
  improves reasoning. The copy task can be solved
  without iterative reasoning.

  Conclusion:
    Tesseract decouples trainable parameter count from
    recursive computation depth: the same 210,832 parameter
    model can execute the shared transformer block 1, 2, 4,
    or 8 times, increasing computation without introducing
    additional trainable parameters.
